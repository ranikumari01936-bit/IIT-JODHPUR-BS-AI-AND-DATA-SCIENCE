Correlation का मतलब होता है दो variables के बीच संबंध (relationship) को मापना। यह बताता है कि एक variable बदलने पर दूसरा variable कैसे बदलता है।

Positive correlation: अगर income बढ़े तो spending भी बढ़े।

Negative correlation: अगर price बढ़े तो demand घटे।

Zero correlation: variables का कोई सीधा संबंध नहीं।

Example (Simple):
मान लो आपके पास data है:

Study hours ↑ → Exam marks ↑ → Positive correlation

TV watching hours ↑ → Exam marks ↓ → Negative correlation

👉 Correlation सिर्फ linear relationship बताता है, कारण (cause) नहीं। यानी अगर दो चीज़ें correlated हैं, इसका मतलब यह नहीं कि एक दूसरे का कारण है।

High correlation ka matlab hai ki do variables ke beech bahut strong linear relationship hai.

variance, mean, median, and mode help us understand data:

1. Mean (Average)
Formula: 
Mean
=
Sum of all values
Number of values

Use: Shows the central tendency (typical value).

Limitation: Sensitive to outliers (e.g., one very large number can pull the mean up).

2. Median (Middle value)
Arrange data in ascending order → pick the middle value.

Use: Represents the "middle" of the dataset.

Advantage: Not affected by extreme values (outliers).

Example: Salaries → median is often better than mean because one billionaire can distort the average.

3. Mode (Most frequent value)
The value that occurs most often.

Use: Useful for categorical data (e.g., most common shoe size).

Can also show peaks in numerical data distributions.

How they work together:
Mean tells the "average" value.

Median tells the "middle" value (robust against outliers).

Mode tells the "most common" value.

Variance tells how consistent or spread out the data is.

👉 Example (Exam Marks):

Mean = 70 → average score.

Median = 72 → middle student scored 72.

Mode = 75 → most students scored 75.

Variance = 20 → scores are fairly close to the mean (not too spread out).

The Coefficient of Variation (CV) is a statistical measure that tells us how much variation (spread) there is in a dataset relative to its mean.

📌 Why it helps
Relative measure of variability: Unlike variance or standard deviation (which are absolute), CV compares spread relative to the average.

Unit-free: Since it’s a ratio, CV can be used to compare datasets measured in different units.

Interpretation:

Low CV → data is consistent (less spread around mean).

High CV → data is more scattered (less reliable).
